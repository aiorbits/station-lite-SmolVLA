# Historical handoff: 4 November 2026

English publication summary of the initial investigation. This is historical context, not the current deployment state. The complete original investigation record is retained locally.

## Task and platform

The initial task was a right-arm pick-and-place from a white grid to a brown target area. The red wooden block was the grasp object. ACT had demonstrated successful execution using the existing robot and recordings. The project investigated whether pretrained SmolVLA could adapt to the platform using the same local data.

Hans22 provided an RTX 4080 Laptop policy/training host. The robot was ido, using the vendor SDK, CAN and separate camera service. Network addresses were operational examples, not permanent identity guarantees. Original data comprised 45 episodes and 16,936 frames at nominal 30Hz, with three 640×480 cameras and no real per-frame acquisition timestamps.

## Initial models and interfaces

The early 14D model represented both arms: measured joint/gripper state and absolute EEF/gripper action targets. It was trained through 20,000 steps. A new right7 view selected only the right arm and top/right-wrist cameras; its candidate was independently fine-tuned for 10,000 updates. Seven output values meant EEF pose plus gripper, not seven joint positions.

The split was fixed at 35 training episodes and 10 diagnostic holdout episodes. Earlier ACT comparisons and subsequent inspection mean the holdout was not an untouched final benchmark. Normalization statistics and checkpoint processors were integral to each model artifact.

## Observed plateau and timing

The 14D model lifted the arm from Home and then remained in an early workspace region. Twelve installed chunks did not establish task progress or future closure. Typical later measured EEF positions remained around X158–168mm, Y9–17mm, Z226–232mm. Raw predictions also remained far from demonstrated grasp-stage targets, so execution guards alone did not explain the plateau.

An observation-phase bug was found: a nominal wait of approximately 6.67 seconds could be treated as complete in less than one second. Later clock/phase corrections restored the intended observation timing. Timing correction did not by itself solve policy progression.

Frozen-observation GPU comparisons and paired image/state replacement experiments showed strong sensitivity to the 14D robot state. Replacing the live state with a demonstrated state improved predicted progress more than replacing images alone. This motivated the right7 experiment; it did not prove a unique general root cause.

## Initial right7 IK rejection

The first right7 hardware preflight rejected all 50 targets. Some required substantial retreat to solve and later targets had no solution. That failure occurred before a vendor trajectory dry-run or policy publishing. SDK construction nevertheless enabled motors. The original failure lacked a complete saved observation/prediction, preventing exact replay.

The plan prioritized SDK lifecycle inspection, saving failure inputs before checking, FK–IK round trips with matched frame/TCP/seed, representation/normalization auditing, then continuous IK and vendor planning. Known-pose feasibility and solver success had to be distinguished from soft-limit and continuity acceptance. Later investigation superseded this historical failure; it is not the new9 baseline status.

## Data and semantic lessons

The red object and brown placement region were initially insufficiently explicit in task text. The right7 view adopted a precise task string. Some legacy target identifiers encoded starting-layout suffixes rather than destination semantics; new metadata must separate source cell, grasp-object color/type and placement target.

Preserve original records and review complete trajectories. Use original frame indices for alignment, close command, measured closure and lift. Failure examples belong in the diagnostic archive, not silently in an expert-success training set. Old success labels are not substitutes for a fresh manual quality audit.

## Safety and reproducibility principles carried forward

Use CUDA for model computations and stop if unavailable. Keep exact source/checkpoint/processor identity. Never bypass failed guards by enlarging limits or forcing closure. Constructor side effects, Home movement and cleanup behavior must be explicit. An isolated SDK dry-run is distinct from a live connection with enabled motors.
